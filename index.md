Hello, I am Toby Benjamin Clark, a doctoral researcher at the [University of Nottingham](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/), based in the [Computational Optimisation and Learning Lab](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/research/groups/col/).

My research sits at the intersection of combinatorial optimisation and formal methods. Currently, I am interested in the use of [Satisfiability Modulo Theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satisfiability_modulo_theories) for the synthesis and verification of provably-correct pruning rules in machine sequencing problems.

I am fully-funded by the [University of Nottingham](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/) under a studentship award, supervised by [Dr. Jason Atkin](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/computerscience/people/jason.atkin) and [Dr. Geert De Maere](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/computerscience/people/geert.de_maere). Before starting my PhD, I worked on the application of dependent type theories to natural language semantics and automated program verification.

---

Outside of academia, I have experience as both an industry consultant and software engineer, focusing on the integration of mathematical programming into real-world systems, including heat pump optimisation, electric vehicle routing, and biodiversity credit trading.

In my spare time, I regularly compete in competitions, hackathons and pursue independent projects, some of which are collected in [Oddments](https://tobybenjaminclark.github.io/wiki/oddments/). These give me opportunities to explore topical areas of computer science; infact, many of these projects have won awards, and one was even featured on [BBC World](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/cgqy2dypdw0t?post=asset%3A81099280-9f40-49a9-99e2-8e49f94df069#post).

If you would like to get in touch with me, please feel free to email me at toby.clark@nottingham.ac.uk.
